## Market map in 5 lines

1. Government pays roughly three-quarters of a sector one consumer guide puts at about $36bn a year (*secondary estimate*); the rest comes from older people's contributions.
2. Home care was rebuilt on 1 Nov 2025 as Support at Home: 338,049 people receiving care through 950 providers at 31 March 2026, with about 100,000 more waiting.
3. Money now moves claim by claim, so the pain is paperwork: invoices, agreed prices, evidence, statements. A provider group told the Senate about 31 minutes of admin per hour of care.
4. Platforms (ShiftCare $8–$20/user/month, Lookout, AlayaCare, Visualcare) and new compliance tools are crowding the provider side; Mable takes 16.6% between client and worker.
5. Pay-a-lot-and-still-complain: self-managed providers (Trilogy Care, HomeMade) and Mable, where workers and families report rejected and late-paid invoices.

## Idea 1 (rank 1): PaidFirstTime

**Pitch:** An invoice builder for independent care workers, cleaners, gardeners and small suppliers that produces invoices a Support at Home provider will accept the first time.

**Gap it fills:** Under self-managed Support at Home, a worker's invoice has to carry the official service name, one line per service with date, duration and cost, and an ABN and GST status that match the business register. Providers reject anything else. Tools exist for the provider (Invoice Harbour), for the family (Wayly) and for NDIS workers (Earni). I found nothing built for the worker under Support at Home.

**Who has the pain:** Sole-trader support workers, cleaners, gardeners and allied health with 2 to 20 self-managed aged care clients, in the week an invoice is rejected or a payment is a month late. Second buyer: the family member who self-manages and is losing carers.

**Proof (search excerpts, unverified at source):**
1. "My poor support worker has not been paid for 2 months. I have lost 2 carers due to late/non payment of invoices." [Trustpilot, Trilogy Care](https://au.trustpilot.com/review/trilogycare.com.au)
2. "This provider says it will pay invoices in 7 to 10 days but I have been waiting a month" [Trustpilot, HomeMade Support](https://au.trustpilot.com/review/homemadesupport.com.au)
3. "If you use different names, your invoice might be delayed or sent back to you." [Trilogy Care services guide](https://trilogycare.com.au/uploads/services-explained.pdf)

Supporting: Trilogy's public reply blamed a supplier for "submitting invoices that didn't match our system requirements"; a reviewer reported six staff giving six different rejection reasons (paraphrase); HelloCare reported workers unpaid for over a month ([24 Nov 2025](https://hellocare.com.au/support-at-home-chaos-leaves-home-care-workers-waiting-weeks-for-payment/)).

**Pain score: 21/25.** Frequency 4, intensity 5 (income withheld for weeks), willingness to pay 3 (workers already lose 10% to Mable; untested for a standalone tool), reachability 4 (Facebook groups, Mable worker communities), buildability 5.

**Closest competitors and why they miss:**
- [Earni](https://earni.au/): sole-trader invoicing built around the NDIS price guide; no Support at Home mention found (open question).
- [Invoice Harbour](https://invoiceharbour.com.au/self-managed-invoicing): codes invoices after they arrive, sold to providers.
- [Wayly](https://wayly.com.au/): checks invoices for families before they pay.
- [Mable](https://mable.com.au/help_centre/a-guide-to-fees-on-mable/): handles invoicing but costs 16.6% combined and reviewers report two-week delays.
- Xero, Hnry, generic templates: no service list, no provider rules.

**Pricing guess (estimate):** $12/month or $99/year per worker, free for the first 3 invoices. Reasoning: a worker on $55/hour pays Mable about $5.50 per hour; $12 a month is about two hours of that fee, and one avoided late payment covers a year.

**MVP:**
- *Core feature:* pick the provider, pick services from the official Support at Home service list, enter date, hours and rate, get a PDF that passes a rule check (one line per service, quarter-hour units, ABN and GST checked against ABN Lookup, no bundled lines, no per-km transport where the provider bans it).
- *Skip:* payments, accounting, tax, rostering, a mobile app, NDIS.
- *Stack:* Softr or Glide front end on Airtable, Make for the ABN Lookup call and PDF generation (Documint or PDFMonkey), Stripe payment link. *Estimated* cost under $150/month.
- *14 days:* Days 1–2 collect the invoice rules of Trilogy Care, HomeMade and three other self-managed providers and the official service list. Days 3–5 Airtable base and rule checks. Days 6–8 form and PDF. Days 9–10 test with 5 real workers on real invoices. Days 11–12 fix what got rejected. Day 13 Stripe and landing page. Day 14 post to groups.

**First 10 customers:** Facebook groups for independent support workers and self-managed home care; the Aged Care Worker Support Group named by HelloCare; reviewers on the Trilogy Care and HomeMade Trustpilot pages; local cleaners and gardeners advertising "Support at Home" on Google and Gumtree.

**DM:** "Hi, I saw you work with self-managed Support at Home clients. Have you had an invoice bounced or paid late this year? I'm building a simple tool that formats invoices the way Trilogy and HomeMade require so they go through first time. Could I format your next invoice for free and see if it gets paid faster?"

**Biggest risk:** Providers fix this themselves with a supplier portal, or the late payments are caused by provider backlogs rather than invoice format. **Cheapest test this week:** hand-format 10 real invoices for 5 workers in a spreadsheet template and count how many are paid within 10 days.

### WHAT WOULD KILL THIS IDEA?
- Earni or Mable already do Support at Home invoices properly (check Earni first; 10 minutes).
- Trilogy Care or HomeMade launch a portal where workers key invoices in directly.
- Workers say delays continue even with perfect invoices.
- Fewer than 3 of 10 workers will pay $12 after the free invoices.
- A big player could copy the formatter in a weekend. The defence is the per-provider rule library and being where the workers already are, which is thin.

## Idea 2 (rank 2): AT-HM Pack

**Pitch:** A form that turns an occupational therapist's recommendation and a supplier's quote into one complete evidence pack for assistive technology and home modification claims.

**Gap it fills:** AT-HM claims need a prescription or written recommendation, the item's classification code, quotes (at least two for some work), an agreed price and proof of delivery. The Department's first review found only 23% of AT-HM claims had enough evidence of agreed price and 50% enough evidence of delivery. A search for prescription templates, code lookups or pack builders for this scheme found none.

**Who has the pain:** Independent and small-practice occupational therapists doing aged care home assessments, and small mobility-equipment suppliers, at the moment a provider sends the paperwork back or sits on an invoice.

**Proof (search excerpts, unverified at source):**
1. Suppliers are "doing more work than ever" on quotes and information, and the process is "quite a problematic one" — Serena Ovens, CEO, Assistive Technology Suppliers Australia. [Australian Ageing Agenda](https://www.australianageingagenda.com.au/technology/at-hm-rollout-problematic-unnecessarily-complex/)
2. "My dad needs this rollator for his neuropathy and I have been given one excuse after another." [Trustpilot, HomeMade Support](https://au.trustpilot.com/review/homemadesupport.com.au)
3. Official finding: 23% of AT-HM claims had sufficient evidence of agreed price. [Department of Health Cycle 1 report, 24 Sep 2026](https://www.health.gov.au/resources/publications/support-at-home-payment-integrity-rolling-review-cycle-1-summary-report)

Supporting: 65,402 people were waiting for AT-HM funding at 30 June 2026 ([Ageing Agenda](https://theageingagenda.com.au/executive/policy/wait-list-for-sah-reaches-106977/)); Occupational Therapy Australia has surveyed members for about six months and reports inconsistency and lack of clarity (paraphrase).

**Pain score: 19/25.** Frequency 4, intensity 4, willingness to pay 3 (therapists bill report time, so faster reports are worth money; untested), reachability 4 (two peak bodies, therapist Facebook groups), buildability 4.

**Closest competitors and why they miss:**
- Care platforms such as [Lookout](https://support.thelookoutway.com/en/articles/at-hm-under-support-at-home): handle the claim line and admin fee, not the therapist's or supplier's documents.
- [didyougo](https://didyougo.com.au/post/what-counts-as-evidence-of-service-delivery-support-at-home): evidence of service visits, not equipment.
- Therapy practice software (Halaxy, Splose, Cliniko): general report templates, no AT-HM list or tier logic.
- [Trilogy Care's OT service](https://trilogycare.com.au/need-ot-for-at-hm-get-the-assessment-you-need): sells assessments, not a tool.

**Pricing guess (estimate):** $29/month per therapist or $5 per pack; $79/month per supplier branch. Reasoning: if a pack saves 20 minutes of report time, one pack a month pays for it at typical therapy rates.

**MVP:**
- *Core feature:* guided form (client need, item from the AT-HM list with code and prescription category, tier, quotes, agreed price, delivery confirmation link the client or family taps) producing one PDF pack.
- *Skip:* integrations with care platforms, ordering, payments, clinical notes.
- *Stack:* Fillout or Tally form, Airtable holding the AT-HM list, Make and a PDF generator, Stripe.
- *14 days:* Days 1–3 load the official AT-HM list and guidelines into Airtable. Days 4–7 form and PDF. Days 8–10 three therapists run real cases. Days 11–12 revise. Days 13–14 supplier quote add-on and landing page.

**First 10 customers:** Occupational Therapy Australia aged care interest group, therapist Facebook groups, LinkedIn search for "aged care occupational therapist" sole traders, Assistive Technology Suppliers Australia member directory.

**DM:** "Hi, I'm talking to OTs doing AT-HM work under Support at Home. How often does a provider bounce your recommendation or ask for more paperwork? I've built a form that produces the full pack (recommendation, code, quotes, agreed price, delivery sign-off) in one PDF. Would you try it on your next case, free?"

**Biggest risk:** The bottleneck is funding and waitlists, not paperwork, so better packs don't speed anything up. **Cheapest test this week:** ask 10 therapists how many AT-HM recommendations were sent back in the last 3 months and why.

### WHAT WOULD KILL THIS IDEA?
- Therapists say rework is rare and the delay is all on the funding side.
- The Department publishes a mandatory standard form (the sector is asking for clearer guidelines).
- AT-HM volumes keep falling (spend is reported about 30% down).
- Providers each insist on their own form and won't accept a generic pack.
- A practice-software vendor could add a template quickly; this is a feature-sized product.

## Idea 3 (rank 3): Price Receipt

**Pitch:** A text-message link that records a participant agreeing to a price before the service happens, and files a time-stamped receipt the provider can hand to the Department's reviewers.

**Gap it fills:** The Department reviewed 2,448 claims from 60 providers and found only 35% had enough evidence of an agreed price. It now asks providers to document the price with the participant before a service or item is booked. The rolling review covers all providers. Platforms store service agreements, but agreements go stale with every price change, new service or third-party purchase.

**Who has the pain:** Finance or quality managers at small and mid-sized Support at Home providers (under about 500 clients), when the review notice arrives or prices change. There are about 950 providers in total, so this is a small market sold at a higher price.

**Proof (search excerpts, unverified at source):**
1. "just 35 per cent of all claims had sufficient evidence of agreed price" [The Ageing Agenda](https://theageingagenda.com.au/executive/policy/evidence-gaps-in-sah-delivery/)
2. Cycle 1 "identified significant gaps in the documentary evidence submitted by the providers" [Department summary report](https://www.health.gov.au/sites/default/files/2026-09/support-at-home-payment-integrity-rolling-review-cycle-1-summary-report.pdf)
3. One provider told the Senate inquiry its head office spent an estimated extra 320 hours a week on claiming alone (paraphrase). [The Weekly Source](https://www.theweeklysource.com.au/pearl-home-care-takes-support-at-home-double-payment-nightmare-to-senate-inquiry/)

**Honest caveat:** this is regulator data, not customers asking for a product. Nobody was found saying "I wish there was". That is why it ranks third despite the score.

**Pain score: 20/25 on paper.** Frequency 5 (65% of claims failed), intensity 4, willingness to pay 3 (unproven), reachability 4 (public provider register), buildability 4.

**Closest competitors and why they miss:**
- Lookout, AlayaCare, ShiftCare, Visualcare: service agreements and e-signatures at onboarding; no per-change price confirmation found (not verified with vendors).
- didyougo: delivery evidence. [Governa AI](https://www.governa.ai/blog-posts/support-at-home-monthly-statements): statements.
- Kismet (funded, $8.2m): fraud and non-compliance detection, sold upmarket.
- Consultants and law firms: one-off audits.

**Pricing guess (estimate):** $149/month up to 200 participants, $399/month up to 1,000. Reasoning: priced below one day of consultant time per month, above ShiftCare's $5 per client add-on only for the smallest providers.

**MVP:**
- *Core feature:* upload a CSV of participants and prices, send each a link by text or email, capture agree/decline with timestamp, export an evidence register and per-participant PDFs.
- *Skip:* integrations, claiming, statements, anything clinical.
- *Stack:* Airtable, Softr portal, Make, Twilio text messages, PDF generator, Stripe.
- *14 days:* Days 1–2 read the Cycle 1 report and regulatory bulletin RB 2026-1 for what counts as evidence. Days 3–7 build send, capture and register. Days 8–10 pilot with one provider's 20 participants. Days 11–14 fix, add export, write the one-page explainer.

**First 10 customers:** Small providers on the My Aged Care provider list, Ageing Australia state network events, LinkedIn "quality and compliance manager" at home care providers, home care consultants who could resell.

**DM:** "Hi, the Department's first payment integrity review found only 35% of claims had evidence of an agreed price. How are you recording price agreement when rates change? I have a simple text-link tool that captures it and builds the evidence file. Could I run it for 20 of your clients free?"

**Biggest risk:** Platforms ship this as a feature, or older participants don't tap text links. **Cheapest test this week:** call 10 small providers and ask whether they have had a review notice and what evidence they supplied.

### WHAT WOULD KILL THIS IDEA?
- Lookout or ShiftCare already do per-change price confirmation (ask their sales teams; one hour).
- Failing the review turns out to carry no real penalty.
- Many participants can't or won't confirm by phone, so staff still collect paper.
- Providers won't put participant data in a new vendor's hands without security certification.
- This is the easiest of the three for a big vendor to copy.
